**How to use this.** It explains words, not answers. Question 1 is a reading check, not legal
advice: does the excerpt say what the answer says? Simpler words are fine if the meaning is the
same. Dropping or changing a limit, condition or exception is not, if that changes the meaning.

### Words that change the meaning

| Contract wording | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| shall, will, must | has to |
| may | is allowed to, but doesn't have to |
| shall not, may not | isn't allowed to |
| provided that, subject to | but only if; this is limited by |
| notwithstanding X | despite X; this rule beats X |
| except, other than, excluding | everything apart from this |
| in no event | never |
| including, without limitation | these are examples, not the full list |
| herein, hereof, hereunder | in this agreement |
| thereof, therein | of or in the thing just mentioned |
| the foregoing | what was just said |
| Party, the parties | one of the signers; the signers |
| Affiliate | a company linked by ownership or control |
| Term | how long the agreement lasts |
| without cause, for convenience | for any reason; no fault needed |
| for cause | because the other side did something wrong |
| breach | failing to do what the agreement requires |
| cure | fix a breach within the allowed time |
| indemnify, hold harmless | pay for the other side's losses or claims |
| consent not unreasonably withheld | they must have a fair reason to say no |
| successors and assigns | whoever takes over a party's place |

### The 15 clause types

| Clause type | What it means |
|---|---|
| Renewal Term | Whether the agreement extends after the first period. "Automatically renews" means it continues unless someone gives notice. |
| Expiration Date | When the agreement ends, or how long the first period lasts. |
| Termination For Convenience | Ending the agreement without needing a reason, usually after giving notice. |
| Post-Termination Services | Duties that continue after the end, such as support, sell-off or transition help. |
| Audit Rights | One party's right to inspect the other's books or records. |
| Exclusivity | A party is the only one allowed to do something, often limited to a territory, product or period. "Non-exclusive" means others may do it too. |
| Non-Compete | A limit on a party competing with the other, often for a set time or area. |
| Minimum Commitment | The least a party must buy, sell or pay. |
| Revenue/Profit Sharing | How income is split. A royalty is a payment for using something, often a percentage of sales. |
| Insurance | Cover a party must carry, often with minimum amounts. |
| Warranty Duration | How long a promise about quality or performance lasts. |
| Uncapped Liability | Harm that the limit on damages does not apply to. These usually appear as exceptions to a limit. |
| Change Of Control | What happens if a party is bought or merged, or ownership shifts. |
| Anti-Assignment | Limits on handing the agreement to someone else, often needing the other side's consent. |
| Governing Law | Which state's or country's law applies. "Venue" or "jurisdiction" means where disputes are heard, which is a different thing. |

### Damages words

- **Direct damages:** losses caused straight away by the breach.
- **Consequential or indirect damages:** knock-on losses, such as lost profits.
- **Limitation of liability:** a cap on how much a party can be made to pay.
- **Gross negligence:** extreme carelessness, worse than ordinary negligence.
- **Willful misconduct:** deliberate wrongdoing.
